# Research: Why does almost all of Egypt live along the Nile?

> **Corrected after the fact-check** (`fact-check.md`). The claims below carry the corrections. Do not reuse these errors of the first version:
>
> - The "close to 99%" covers the Nile Valley and Delta (`countrystudies.us/egypt/48.htm`), **not the river banks only**, and it is an estimate, not a count.
> - FAO's "over 96%" means "across", not "more than". With 4%, "more than 96%" would pass 100%.
> - The 1,492 per km2 and "about 2 per km2" rest on one FAO publication, paired with 1994 and 1995 population figures. **Not for narration:** say "around fifteen hundred" and "a tiny fraction of that".
> - NASA dates its image and caption to 2012, **not the 97% figure**. Say "published in 2012".
> - The valley and Delta are the one *large* part with reliable water, **not the only one:** FAO reports groundwater irrigation in Matrouh, Sinai and the New Valley.
> - The lake's size varies by account: 300 x 10 miles, 340 miles long, 311 miles long and 6.2 wide on average. **Not for narration.**
> - The AQUASTAT 2005 PDF is readable page by page, and it was read: "about 4 percent" and "about 97 percent" together.
> - The 7.7% inhabited is one of several counts (4.8%, about 10.5%, 13.7%), undated and undefined by UNICEF. **Not for narration.**
> - **Over 99% in the 1937 census (Kiser): one source only, not for narration.** The owner removed it from the script. It is not a basis for a trend: no opened source reports one.

## Central question
Why does almost all of Egypt live along the Nile? Because the Nile Valley and Delta are the one large part of a roughly 1,000,000 km2 country with reliable water: the rest is mostly desert with very little rain, and the river brings in water, and once brought silt, from rain falling far to the south in Ethiopia. (Interpretation built from C01-C12; the pieces are sourced, the sentence is ours.)

## Contested points

- **Share of land and people.** The land share is 3.5% (S04, 1990), 4% (S03, 1993-94) and under 5% (S01, 2012). The population share is "close to 99%" (S04, 1990 estimate, valley and Delta) and "roughly 97%" (S01, published 2012). These differ by definition and date, not necessarily by error. Weight of evidence: "about 4%" and "about 97%" are safe round figures. Open: which one is correct for today; no census was opened.
- **Inhabited area: 4% or 7.7%.** UNICEF (S02) says 7.7% of the area is inhabited, wider than the valley and Delta alone, which suggests it counts oases, the Red Sea and Sinai coasts and newer desert settlements. A search snippet said so too, but the UNICEF page does not define it. Weight: favors "valley and Delta are about 4%", and 7.7% as a broader count. Open on the definition.
- **Ethiopian share: 86% or less.** S05 and S06 say 86%; S06 gives the Blue Nile alone as 60-62%, S07 says 58% (Blue Nile) plus 14% (Atbara) = 72% without the Sobat. A 2024 NBI outlook said about 80% for the highlands; it appeared only in a search snippet and was not opened. Weight: "most of the water, around 86%, by a common estimate, from Ethiopia" is supportable; use "the Blue Nile alone more than half".
- **Length of the Nile.** English Wikipedia gives 7,088 km (Liu 2009 satellite measurement); older references give about 6,650 km. Not opened beyond Wikipedia; the length is not used. Open.
