Look at a map of China, and one thing stands out. In the west, the Tibetan Plateau rises nearly five kilometers into the sky. In the north, the Gobi Desert stretches for a thousand miles.

And in between, two great rivers carve their way east, toward the sea. For more than 2,000 years, this landscape has pushed its people together. But in 1644, a small army from beyond the Great Wall would test that unity like never before.
