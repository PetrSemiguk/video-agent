# Facts ledger: Why China keeps coming back together, and Europe never did

Script: `script.md` (draft of 2026-11-07, **not approved, no TTS**). Research: `research.md`
(pass of 2026-11-07). Claim ids (`C01`-`C40`) and contested-point ids (`CP1`-`CP9`) are that
file's. Every factual sentence below cites one. No fact in the script comes from anywhere else.

One rule from the research is applied throughout: a claim marked "1 source" there is **never
spoken as flat fact**. In the script it carries an in-sentence attribution — "one study", "one
count", "one tally", "on one reading", "on one account", "one recent study", or a named author.
Those words are load-bearing; removing them breaks the ledger.

## Things deliberately not said

- **Any count of European states.** Tilly's series is quoted as 500 in 1500 falling to 25, 30 or 20; the
  common "about 200 in 1900" traces to nothing (`research.md`, "Could not verify"). Euratlas's counts are
  single-source and its data are not licensed for our use. The script therefore shows frames and says
  "dozens" and "more lines, not fewer".
- **The area of the North China Plain**, the length of the steppe frontier, and any length of the Great
  Wall. All untraced or single-source and undated.
- **A rainfall line on the map.** The wording of 106 is supported by two sources; no isohyet is drawn.
- **The Holy Roman Empire's units as borders.** No allowed source maps them; 86-90 use a counter instead.
- **China's and Europe's populations**, and any claim that their population histories match. Nothing in
  `research.md` supports it.
- **Exam origin and abolition dates**, the 1648 count of imperial territories, Zheng He, Latin as a
  unifying medium, and the Black Death: all dropped in research for want of an opened source.
